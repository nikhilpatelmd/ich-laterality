Priors

Prior Distribution Definitions

In defining prior distributions, we generally followed suggestions from previous literature.1–3 Specifically, we defined 4 sets of priors for each outcome:

Neutral prior: This prior assumed that hemispheric lateralization did not significantly affect the primary outcome and represents the null hypothesis. It is centered around a mean of 0 and standard deviation of 0.35, corresponding to OR of 1 and 95% of the probability mass for the OR between 0.5 to 1.99.

Left hemisphere prior: This prior assumes that the primary and secondary outcomes are more likely with left hemisphere injury, centering around a mean of -0.22 and standard deviation of 0.175 which corresponds to an OR of 0.8 and 95% of the probability mass for the OR between 0.57 to 1.13.

Right hemisphere prior: This prior assumes that the primary and secondary outcomes are more likely with right hemisphere injury, centering around a mean of 0.18 and standard deviation of 0.175 which corresponds to an OR of 1.2 and 95% of the probability mass for the OR between 0.85 to 1.69.

Flat prior: This prior most mimics a frequentist analysis in that the prior distribution is so wide that it essentially contributes no information to the posterior model. It is centered around a mean of 0 and standard deviation of standard deviation of 5 which corresponds to an OR of 1 and 95% of the probability mass for the OR between 0 to 1.8033745^{4}.
